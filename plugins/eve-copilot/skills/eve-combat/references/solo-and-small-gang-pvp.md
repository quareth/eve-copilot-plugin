# Solo and small-gang PvP

Use this reference for solo, duo, microgang, and small-gang hunting or defense.
Also read the environment reference for the selected space and current activity.

## The solo and small-gang contract

Small groups lack some fleet roles, so each ship must cover a deliberate share
of scouting, tackle, damage, application, survival, capacitor, mobility, and
escape. Define which functions are self-provided and which are supplied by
companions. Do not fit every ship as an isolated solo ship when the gang has
specialists.

Before choosing the hull, establish:

- hunting, baiting, defending, camping, capturing, stealing, or escaping;
- desired target class and targets to decline;
- brawl, scram-kite, web-kite, long-point kite, sniping, cloaked attack, or
  flexible engagement style;
- gate-to-gate travel, staged defense, filament roam, wormhole chain, or fixed
  objective;
- expected scout, links, logistics, EWAR, probes, interdictors, or cyno support;
- acceptable pod/implant and ship loss.

## Solo archetypes

### Brawler

Needs a credible way to land and hold scram/web range, survive initial damage,
apply under high transverse velocity, and manage neut or reload pressure. Assess
who controls range before comparing paper damage. Name the ships, control
effects, neuts, or escape tools that invalidate the plan.

### Scram kiter

Needs speed and control inside scram range, useful damage beyond the opponent's
best brawl range, and a plan against dual webs, neuts, drones, and faster hulls.
Define the intended orbit/manual range and ammunition change.

### Long-point or web kiter

Needs sustainable point range, speed, projection, application, sensor range,
and escape margin. Account for links, heat, slingshots, damps, long webs,
projection, fast tackle, grid geometry, and gate/objective commitment.

### Bait or counter-engagement

Optimize for surviving the expected reveal window and signaling support, not
for appearing strong on paper. State the support arrival time, cyno or tackle
dependency, cap pressure, and failure condition. Do not recommend bait without
an actual escalation or extraction plan.

### Hunter, scanner, or cloaked attacker

Balance probe/cloak/cyno utility with combat capability, recalibration delay,
lock time, tackle, target selection, and escape. Distinguish hunting support
from a ship expected to finish the target alone.

## Small-gang composition

Allocate roles explicitly:

- scout and initial tackle;
- secondary/heavy tackle and anti-MJD control;
- damage and damage-type coverage;
- webs, paints, damps, tracking disruption, ECM, or neuts;
- command bursts;
- logistics or remote support;
- probes and combat scanning;
- bubbles or bubble counterplay where legal;
- anti-tackle and extraction;
- cyno or escalation control when relevant.

Check that tackle range, damage projection, logistics range, anchor speed, and
warp speed are compatible. A fast damage wing that outruns its logistics or a
long-range gang with only scram tackle is not coherent.

## Target and engagement policy

For the chosen fit, describe:

- favorable target archetypes and why the control/application plan works;
- dangerous archetypes, specific mechanics, and signs of hidden support;
- required starting range or initial state;
- ammunition, script, heat, drone, and capacitor opening state;
- disengage conditions before escape is lost;
- what changes if the opponent has links, implants, faction tackle, neuts,
  tracking/application support, or a cyno.

Do not say a hull “counters” another without conditions. A matchup changes with
range, fits, piloting, grid, security rules, heat, skills, and support.

## Lowsec and factional warfare

Resolve the exact complex, battlefield, campaign site, or insurgency objective.
Verify current hull class, faction, fleet-size, gate, weather/effect, and scoring
restrictions before fitting. Do not assume all FW complexes use the same entry
rules, and do not carry seasonal Military Campaign restrictions into ordinary
sites without verification.

Lowsec has no ordinary warp disruption bubbles, but gates, stations, weapons
timers, sentry guns, suspect/criminal consequences, smartbomb exposure, and
capital escalation still affect fitting and engagement. Check legality and
security-status consequences separately from mechanical feasibility.

For capture play, decide whether success means holding the beacon, contesting
the timer, killing entrants, escaping unfavorable entries, or moving quickly
between sites. Those lead to different priorities for speed, scan resolution,
range control, tank, and ammunition endurance.

## Nullsec roaming and objective intrusion

Assume bubbles and interdiction are possible. Determine whether the ship needs
nullification, cloak/MWD travel behavior, probe capability, fast align, bubble
burning speed, MJD, filament extraction, or a scout. Nullification is conditional
and must be checked against current module/hull behavior.

For ESS or Skyhook work, use the fleet-objective reference even with a small
gang. Their local restrictions and commitment mechanics override generic roam
fits.

For filament roams, include filament eligibility, regroup behavior, return or
extraction policy, cargo limits, and the possibility that the fleet cannot
immediately leave together.

## Gate camps and piracy

Separate camp roles: eyes/scout, fast locker, tackle, decloak, damage,
interdiction, anti-crash control, EWAR, logistics, looter, and counter-drop
security. Model gate guns and legality in lowsec; model bubbles and bombing in
nullsec; model mass, polarization, and rolling control on wormholes.

For escaping a camp, fit against the expected mechanism: instalock, bubble,
scram, web, smartbomb, decloak, drag/stop bubble, combat probes, or pursuit.
There is no one “travel fit” that defeats all camp types.

## Wormhole hunting and brawls

Use pilot/private observations for the chain, hole mass/life, polarization,
occupant activity, and likely response. ESI does not make the chain current.

Ship mass, rolling plan, armor/shield effect, static connection size, refit
access, probes, cloak, cap warfare, polarized weapons, and extraction bookmarks
can dominate hull choice. Every combat group needs a probe-and-exit recovery
plan; do not let the only scanner become an unacknowledged single point of
failure.

For home defense or hole control, define which side controls reinforcements and
mass. For eviction work, use the fleet-objective reference as well.

## Pochven roaming

Treat Pochven as a hybrid objective and travel environment, not ordinary
nullsec. Verify current entry/exit methods, standings gates, NPC interactions,
site effects, and current system rules. Account for player fleets, NPC pressure,
limited extraction, and ammunition/resupply together. Do not infer safety from
low public activity.

## Live response

If the pilot is already in a fight or exposed:

1. lead with the immediate action;
2. state the decisive reason in one line;
3. ask for at most one missing observation such as range, tackle state, Local,
   D-scan, hostile hulls, capacitor, heat, or exit availability;
4. update the engagement policy rather than redesigning the whole fit mid-fight.

If a reported state is ambiguous, choose the action that preserves future
options and say what observation would justify committing.
