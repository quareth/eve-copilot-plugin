# Fleet objectives and capitals

Use this reference for organized doctrines, strategic objectives, logistics,
EWAR, structures, sovereignty, ESS, Skyhooks, Black Ops, and capital warfare.

## Doctrine comes before individual optimization

An FC-supplied doctrine, assigned role, replacement rule, and staging plan are
operational requirements. Retrieve or ask for the exact doctrine and avoid
“improving” one pilot's fit in a way that breaks fleet ranges, tank layer,
resists, mobility, cap chain, ammunition, refit plan, or reimbursement.

Validate the selected character against the doctrine:

- hull and all hard module/rig/drone/charge requirements;
- fitting feasibility with the character's actual skills;
- doctrine-critical support skills such as tank, weapon/application, capacitor,
  targeting, navigation, logistics, EWAR, links, fighters, or capital operation;
- clone, implant, booster, jump clone, and staging assumptions;
- owned hulls, saved fits, replacement stock, and market availability.

Offer deviations only as explicit FC-approval alternatives. If the pilot cannot
meet a critical threshold, recommend the best useful alternate role rather than
quietly fielding a weak version of the primary doctrine.

## Fleet coherence checks

Validate the whole role envelope, not only an individual fit:

- shared tank layer and resist assumptions;
- anchor speed, signature, propulsion, align, warp speed, and range;
- damage projection, ammunition, reload, volley/sustained damage, and target
  calling model;
- logistics repair type/range, lock time, cap chain, incoming alpha margin, and
  jam/damp/neut resilience;
- tackle, webs, paints, damps, tracking disruption, ECM, neuts, and application
  allocation;
- command-burst coverage and compatible movement;
- drones/fighters, assist policy, anti-bomb and anti-drone measures;
- probes, scouts, cynos, bridges, booshes, bubbles, bombing, and extraction;
- standardized module variants, charges, scripts, paste, fuel, and spare hulls.

A role can be mechanically valid and still operationally incompatible with its
fleet. Label those as doctrine failures, not fitting failures.

## Common fleet roles

### Scout and tackle

Prioritize information, initial contact, point/scram application, survival until
support, and communication. Distinguish disposable initial tackle from heavy or
long-range tackle. Account for lock time, sensor range, warp speed, probe/cloak
delay, bubbles, gate guns, and anti-tackle escape.

### Mainline damage

Fit to the FC's engagement and ammunition ranges, target signature, expected
transversal, damage lock/selectability, volley versus sustain, and logistics
anchor. Extra local utility is not valuable if it breaks fleet application,
tank, or replacement standardization.

### Logistics

Define expected incoming alpha and sustained damage, repair type and range,
cap chain or injection, lock time, watch-list/anchor behavior, sensor pressure,
signature/speed, and self-preservation. Include appropriate drones, cap charges,
scripts, ECCM/sensor options, and overheat plan. Do not infer a viable logistics
wing from rep amount alone.

### EWAR and capacitor warfare

Define target priority, required effective range, scripts, falloff, application,
sensor support, survivability, and how many ships must land effects. Neut roles
need an explicit cap budget and target-cycle plan. EWAR fits that cannot remain
inside effect range under the fleet's movement are incoherent.

### Command, boosh, interdiction, and specialist support

Check exact current hull/module restrictions, skill thresholds, burst coverage,
positioning, aggression, bubble legality, spool/delay, ammunition/fuel, and
failure consequences. Specialist fits usually need a simpler fallback role if
their primary mechanic becomes unavailable.

## Strategic-objective fitting

For any objective, separate three requirements:

1. **Eligibility:** can the hull enter, link, entosis, reinforce, damage, repair,
   capture, or contest under current rules?
2. **Objective performance:** can it maintain the required state or damage while
   moving, tanking, and resisting control?
3. **Survival/escalation:** can it live or extract through the opponent's likely
   response, and what support arrives when it cannot?

### Sovereignty and command nodes

Verify current Sovereignty Hub, entosis/link, command-node, ADM, vulnerability,
and capture rules from current official sources. Define hacker/link ship,
defense wing, travel speed across nodes, fitting restrictions while linked,
hostile control tools, and reinforcement response. A high local tank is not a
complete node strategy if the ship cannot move between or control nodes.

### Upwell structures

Verify the exact structure state, vulnerability/repair timer, damage cap,
tether/docking behavior, reinforcement phase, and objective window. Fit around
damage-cap efficiency, fleet survival, cap warfare, fighters, bombs, hostile
escalation, and time on grid. Include ammunition and fuel endurance plus a
retreat or safe-log policy.

### ESS

Verify current ESS access and local-area restrictions before fitting. Generic
nullsec mobility and escape assumptions may not apply inside the encounter.
Define whether the fleet steals, defends, baits, screens the gate, or controls
the exterior grid. Split inside and outside roles when appropriate.

### Orbital Skyhooks

Verify current linking eligibility and restrictions. Treat the linked ship as
an objective platform with constrained movement/escape, then build a defense,
screen, and extraction plan around it. Do not make the link ship the only ship
capable of carrying the objective if loss or interruption ends the plan.

### Highsec wars and structure objectives

Confirm war eligibility, legal targets, War HQ, allies, suspect/limited
engagements, neutral assistance consequences, and structure timers. Highsec
legality does not replace tactical analysis; CONCORD presence does not protect
legal war targets.

## Black Ops and hotdrops

Separate hunter, covert bridge, damage, tackle, EWAR, fuel/logistics, and exit
roles. Define cyno type and eligibility, bridge/jump range, fatigue, fuel,
landing dispersion, decloak/lock timing, target hold time, hostile counter-drop,
and extraction. Current Black Ops balance can change damage and survivability;
refresh recent official changes before recommending expensive fits.

Do not recommend a drop solely because a target appears killable. Require a
counter-escalation and exit assessment.

## Capital and supercapital decisions

Capital hull selection is an escalation and logistics decision before it is a
fitting decision. Establish:

- exact role: anti-capital damage, structure damage, carrier/fighter support,
  FAX logistics, dread siege, supercarrier/titan projection, CRAB PvE, bait, or
  home defense;
- cyno, bridge/jump, range, fatigue, fuel, and jammer constraints;
- siege, triage, industrial or other mode duration and immobility;
- refit, tether, docking, clone, insurance, replacement, and asset-safety plan;
- hostile dread, supercapital, bomber, neut, EWAR, tackle, and escalation risk;
- capacitor warfare and emergency refuel/cap support;
- fighter tubes, fighter roles, spares, control range, and anti-fighter threats;
- extraction after the objective or mode cycle.

Never suggest undocking or committing a capital from an isolated fit comparison.
Require an escalation owner, cyno chain, support fleet, exit condition, and loss
policy. For capital PvE, also read the PvE encounter reference.

## Output additions for fleets

Alongside the exact fit, report:

- assigned role and position relative to anchor/objective;
- doctrine dependencies and assumed fleet support;
- required charges, scripts, drones/fighters, fuel, paste, and replacements;
- operational thresholds that need in-game simulation or FC confirmation;
- escalation and extraction triggers;
- any deviation that requires FC approval.
