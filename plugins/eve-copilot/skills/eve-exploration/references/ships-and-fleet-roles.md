# Ships and fleet roles

Use the active fit, not the hull name, as the route constraint. Query fitting,
asset, skill, and fleet tools before asking the pilot.

## Capability checks

Determine, where exposed:

- probe and analyzer strength and whether the target sites are realistically
  scannable and hackable;
- cloak type, align and warp behavior, and interdiction-nullifier availability;
- tank, capacitor, combat ability, drones, and ammunition logistics;
- cargo capacity, current cargo value, and replacement cost;
- wormhole ship-size and mass consequences.

Do not equate covert cloak with interdiction nullification. Check the fitted
module and its operating constraints. Do not reward tank or combat capability
on an ordinary can route unless it unlocks a target or survival plan.

## Common route biases

| Ship or role | Route bias | Main constraint |
|---|---|---|
| T1 exploration frigate | Short, recoverable high-sec or cautious low-sec trips | Fragile, visible, weak bubble escape |
| Racial covert-ops frigate | Deep pure-exploration coverage in null-sec or wormholes | Fragile; nullifier is fit- and state-dependent |
| Astero | Extended covert roaming with drones or limited defensive options | Higher replacement cost; do not assume nullification |
| Stratios | Shorter hybrid expeditions near extraction or selected hazards | Cruiser exposure, cost, mass, and slower coverage |
| Strategic cruiser | Guarded or combat exploration when the exact fit proves eligible | High loss cost, subsystem dependence, mass and logistics |
| Scout plus combat ship | Scout confirms route and site before heavy-hull commitment | Requires staging, coordination, or reshipping |

Cheap hulls may rationally accept a higher loss probability. Expensive or
massive hulls should demand stronger evidence and shorter exposure. As cargo
value grows, prefer extraction over one more speculative system.

## Fleet planning

Optimize coverage without duplication and commit valuable hulls only after a
scout confirms the route and site.

Useful roles include lead scout, rear or route keeper, scanner/hacker,
combat/guard, mapper/coordinator, and periodic hauler/extractor. One pilot may
hold several roles in a small fleet.

Prefer one of these patterns:

- **Leapfrog:** a scout advances while the group remains on a cleared node.
- **Branch and rejoin:** scanners cover separate branches and return to a rally
  node.
- **Scout then commit:** a cheap or covert hull confirms a guarded site before
  a combat hull travels.
- **Rolling extraction:** periodically remove loot instead of concentrating all
  value in the final return.

Check connection size and remaining mass for every hull, as well as
polarization, regrouping ability, filament capacity, observability, and the
return requirement.
